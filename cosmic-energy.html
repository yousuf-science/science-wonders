💨 The tug of war in the sky

Gravity pulls things together. So a universe full of stars and galaxies should be slowing down — gravity putting the brakes on the expansion. But in 1998 astronomers measured it carefully and got the opposite answer: the universe is speeding up.

Whatever is doing that, we call it dark energy. It acts like a faint push, baked into empty space itself, that gets stronger as there is more space. Today dark energy has won the tug of war, and the universe is flying apart faster and faster.

There is also dark matter — extra invisible stuff that adds gravity but does not glow. You can see its effect: galaxies spin faster than their visible stars would explain, as if surrounded by an unseen halo of mass. The simulation shows those halos as faint violet glows around each galaxy.

🌍 Today, in real numbers

The universe is 13.8 billion years old, expanding at roughly 70 km/s per megaparsec, and bathed in the faint first-light glow at 2.725 K. The mix: about 5% ordinary matter (stars, planets, you), 27% dark matter (the invisible halo), 68% dark energy (the push).

If dark energy keeps doing what it's doing, galaxies will keep drifting further apart. In the very far future the sky will be empty and dark. But that is a very long time from now.

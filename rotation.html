Theory — System of Particles & Rotational Motion (NCERT Class 11, Ch. 7)

1. Centre of Mass

Any system of particles — however oddly shaped, however many parts — has one special point that behaves as though all the mass were concentrated there. That point is the centre of mass. External forces move it as if they were acting on a single particle of total mass.

Rcm = (m₁r₁ + m₂r₂ + …) / (m₁ + m₂ + …)
For a uniform sphere, cube, cylinder — the CoM sits at the geometric centre. For a ring, it sits at the centre — where there is no mass. The CoM does not need to lie inside the body.

This is why a thrown wrench, however wildly it tumbles, has its centre of mass tracing a clean parabola. Gravity acts as if it were pulling that single point.

2. Moment of Inertia

Mass resists being sped up; moment of inertia resists being spun up. Crucially, it depends on where the mass sits relative to the axis. Same mass, further from the axis → much larger I, much harder to spin.

I = Σ mᵢrᵢ²
Standard shapes (mass M, radius R): Ring MR² · Disc / cylinder ½MR² · Solid sphere ⅖MR² · Hollow sphere ⅔MR² · Thin rod about centre (length L) ML²/12

3. Radius of Gyration

The radius of gyration K is the distance at which all the mass could be collapsed into a point (or a ring) without changing the moment of inertia.

I = M·K²
For a disc K = R/√2 ≈ 0.71R. For a ring K = R exactly — all the mass is already at the rim.

4. Parallel Axis Theorem

If you know the moment of inertia about an axis through the centre of mass, the moment about any parallel axis a distance d away is easy:

I = Icm + M·d²
A disc about its centre: ½MR². About the rim (d = R): ½MR² + MR² = 3/2 MR² — three times harder to spin. This is why spinning a hammer by the handle is so different from spinning it by the head.

5. Torque & the Rotational F = ma

Torque is the rotational version of force — a push times its lever-arm. It produces angular acceleration just as force produces linear acceleration.

τ = r × F = I·α
A see-saw balances when the torques cancel: m₁d₁ = m₂d₂. A small mass far out can balance a big mass close in — the principle of every lever, wrench, and door handle.

6. Angular Momentum & its Conservation

For a rotating body, angular momentum L = I·ω. When no external torque acts on the system, L stays constant. Pull the mass in (lower I), and ω must rise to compensate — exactly how a skater spins up, how a diver tucks, and how a collapsing star spins up into a pulsar.

L = I·ω = constant    (when τext = 0)

7. Rolling Motion

A rolling wheel does two things at once — translates and rotates. For rolling without slipping:

v = ω·R
An important consequence: the instantaneous speed of the contact point is zero. The wheel rotates about it for an instant. NCERT 7.32(b) — a True/False question that catches half the class.

The total KE splits between translation and rotation. The more energy hides in rotation, the slower the body accelerates down a ramp:

a = g·sinθ / (1 + I/MR²)
Mass and radius cancel out — only the shape matters. Solid sphere always wins. Ring always loses.

Theory — Why Do Moving Clocks Run Slow?

The Setup

Two twins start at the exact same age. One stays on Earth. The other boards an interstellar starship, accelerates to a fraction of the speed of light $v$, flies to a target destination, turns around, and returns. When they reunite, the traveling twin is measurably younger. This has been verified experimentally with atomic clocks on jets, with GPS satellites, and with subatomic muons.

The Relativistic Math

Special relativity states that the rate at which an inertial clock ticks, as measured by a stationary observer, slows down by the Lorentz factor $\gamma$:

$$\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - \beta^2}}, \quad \text{where } \beta = \frac{v}{c}$$
As $v \to c$, $\beta \to 1$ and $\gamma \to \infty$: time on the traveling ship slows to a standstill relative to Earth.

For a round-trip at constant cruise speed $v$ over a one-way distance $d$, the elapsed time measured by Earth is simply distance divided by speed:

$$t_E = \frac{2d}{v}$$
Earth-frame elapsed time. Independent of time dilation.

The traveler's onboard clock runs slow by a factor of $\gamma$, so the traveler's aging is given by:

$$t_T = \frac{t_E}{\gamma} = \frac{2d}{v} \sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}$$
Traveler-frame elapsed time. This is how many years the astronaut ages during the journey.

The relativistic age difference when they reunite is:

$$\Delta t = t_E - t_T = t_E \left(1 - \frac{1}{\gamma}\right)$$
At $v = 0.99c$, $\gamma \approx 7.09$, meaning the traveler ages only ~14% as much as their twin on Earth.

"Why Isn't It Symmetric?" (The Resolution)

From the traveler's perspective, Earth appears to be moving away and returning. Why doesn't Earth end up younger?

Resolution: The symmetry is broken by the turnaround. To return home, the traveler must fire engines, decelerate, and accelerate back — shifting from one inertial reference frame to another. The Earth twin remains in a single inertial frame throughout. This physical acceleration/frame-jump breaks the symmetry and proves whose clock slowed down.

Experimental Proofs

Hafele–Keating Experiment (1971): Atomic clocks flown around the world on jetliners showed nanosecond-level time shifts matching special and general relativity.

GPS Navigation: Orbiting satellites must correct for ~38 microseconds per day of time dilation. Without relativity corrections, GPS coordinates would drift by ~10 km per day.

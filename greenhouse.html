Theory — What is the Greenhouse Effect?

What is the Greenhouse Effect? (The Simple Version)

Think of a car parked in the sun with its windows rolled up. Sunlight (which is mostly visible light) easily passes through the transparent glass windows and hits the dashboard and seats. These surfaces absorb the sunlight and warm up. Warm objects radiate heat, which takes the form of invisible infrared light. But here is the trick: glass doesn't let infrared light pass through! The heat is trapped inside the car, and the temperature rises dramatically.

The Earth's atmosphere does the exact same thing. Gases like Carbon Dioxide (CO₂), Methane (CH₄), and Water Vapor act like the glass of a greenhouse. They are entirely transparent to the incoming sunlight, letting it pass through to warm the Earth. However, when the warm Earth tries to radiate that heat (infrared) back out into space, these "greenhouse gases" block it, absorb it, and scatter it back down to the surface.

Without this natural effect, Earth would be a frozen, lifeless ball of ice at -18°C. But human activities are adding too much CO₂, effectively making the "glass" thicker and overheating the planet.

Wavelength & Wien's Law

Why is sunlight different from Earth's heat? It depends on temperature. The Sun is incredibly hot (~5,500°C), so it emits high-energy, shortwave radiation (visible light). The Earth is comparatively cold (~15°C), so it emits low-energy, longwave radiation (infrared).

λmax = b / T
Wien's Law: The peak wavelength of light an object emits is inversely proportional to its temperature. Hotter objects emit shorter wavelengths.

Why CO₂? The Quantum View

Our atmosphere is 99% Nitrogen (N₂) and Oxygen (O₂). Why don't they trap heat? N₂ and O₂ are tightly bound symmetric molecules. When an infrared photon hits them, they cannot absorb its specific low energy. CO₂, however, is a polyatomic molecule (O=C=O). Its chemical bonds can bend and stretch asymmetrically. This allows CO₂ to perfectly catch Earth's infrared heat, vibrate vigorously, and re-emit the heat in a random direction.

The Albedo Effect (Reflectivity)

Not all sunlight is absorbed by the Earth. Albedo is a measure of reflectivity. Bright white ice and snow reflect up to 90% of visible light straight back to space before it can warm the ground and become infrared heat. When global temperatures rise and ice melts, dark oceans are exposed. The dark water absorbs more sunlight, creating even more heat—a powerful positive feedback loop.

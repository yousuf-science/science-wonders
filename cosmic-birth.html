The Story In Plain Words

About 13.8 billion years ago, everything you see was packed into a single point. From that one start, space stretched out, cooled down, and slowly grew into stars, planets, oceans — you and me. That moment is called the Big Bang.

🌌 Could there be many universes?

Many serious scientists think yes. The leading idea is called eternal inflation: the same stretching that made our universe never stops in some far-away patches, and each patch becomes its own bubble universe. Other bubbles might have slightly different rules — different forces, different particles, faster or slower stretching.

This isn't fiction. It's a prediction of the same equations that explain the sky. Most of those other universes would be dead: too much stretching rips them apart; too little and they collapse back before anything interesting happens. Only a narrow range of rules makes a long-lived universe like ours.

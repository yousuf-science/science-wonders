Theory — How the Four Laws Make Everything

In 1865, James Clerk Maxwell published the equations that finally unified electricity, magnetism, and light into a single theory. There are four of them. Together, they describe every electromagnetic phenomenon you have ever experienced. Light, radio, X-rays, microwaves, the spark of static, the pull of a fridge magnet, the current in your phone charger. All of it. All four laws.

This simulation lets you play with each law in turn, then assemble them into the grand unification: light is an electromagnetic wave, and its speed falls out of the equations themselves.

⚡ Equation 1 — Gauss's Law for Electricity

Electric charges produce electric fields. The total fluxFlux measures how much of a field "pierces" a surface. Imagine the field as flowing arrows. Flux counts how many cross the surface, weighted by area and direction. of the electric field out of any closed surface equals the charge enclosed inside, divided by the vacuum permittivity ε₀.

∮ E · dA = Q_enclosed / ε₀
E is the electric field vector. dA is a tiny patch of the closed surface, pointing outward. The circle on the integral means we sum over every patch of the closed surface. Q is the total charge sitting inside. ε₀ = 8.854 × 10⁻¹² C²/(N·m²).

The remarkable thing is the shape of the surface does not matter. Stretch it, dent it, balloon it. As long as it encloses the same charge, the total flux is the same. The simulation makes this real. Drag the Gaussian sphere around: the flux number jumps when a charge crosses the boundary and stays flat otherwise.

Punchline: Field lines come out of positive charges and end on negative charges. The strength of the field falls off as 1/r² from a point charge because the same number of field lines spread over a sphere of area 4πr².

🧲 Equation 2 — Gauss's Law for Magnetism

There are no magnetic monopolesA monopole would be a magnetic charge — a lone north or south pole. None has ever been found in nature. Every magnetic field line in the universe is part of a closed loop.. The total magnetic flux out of any closed surface is exactly zero.

∮ B · dA = 0
Every field line that exits a closed surface must also re-enter it. There is no magnetic "source" or "sink".

This is why cutting a bar magnet in half does not give you a north pole and a south pole. It gives you two complete bar magnets, each with its own north and south. The simulation lets you try the cut and watch the field lines re-route themselves.

Punchline: Magnetic field lines always form closed loops. They never start or end. This is the cleanest, shortest of Maxwell's equations, and arguably the most mysterious — every other force in nature has a "charge", but magnetism does not.

🔄 Equation 3 — Faraday's Law of Induction

A changing magnetic flux through a loop induces an EMFElectromotive force. Despite the name, it is not a force but a voltage — the work done per unit charge as it moves around the circuit. Measured in volts. around that loop. The induced EMF equals minus the rate of change of magnetic flux.

ε = −N · dΦ_B / dt where Φ_B = ∫ B · dA
N is the number of turns of wire in the coil. Φ_B is the magnetic flux through one turn. The minus sign is Lenz's law: the induced current always opposes the change that produced it.

Push a bar magnet toward a coil and the coil tries to push it back. Pull the magnet away and the coil tries to pull it back. The coil never welcomes the change. Every generator on Earth (hydroelectric, wind, nuclear, coal) runs on this single principle: spin a coil in a magnetic field and electricity comes out.

Punchline: Electricity and magnetism are not separate things. A magnet that moves becomes an electric source. The "−" sign (Lenz's law) is the universe enforcing conservation of energy: if induction worked the other way, you'd get free energy from a magnet falling toward a coil.

⭕ Equation 4 — Ampère's Law with Maxwell's Correction

Electric currents produce magnetic fields. So far, so Ampère. Maxwell's contribution was to realize that a changing electric field also produces a magnetic field, exactly as if it were a current. He called it the displacement currentNot a real flow of charge, but a "virtual" current carried by a changing electric field. In a charging capacitor, no charge crosses the gap between the plates, but the changing E field there acts magnetically just as if a current did..

∮ B · dℓ = μ₀ ( I_enclosed + ε₀ · dΦ_E / dt )
The left side is the line integral of B around a closed loop (an Amperian loopAn imaginary closed path you draw in space. Ampère's law links the magnetic field along it to the current piercing any surface bounded by it.). I is the real current through the loop. The second term is Maxwell's correction: ε₀ times the rate of change of electric flux. μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A.

Why did Maxwell need to add a term? Consider a charging capacitor. A real current flows in the wire on either side, but no charge physically crosses the gap between the plates. Without Maxwell's correction, Ampère's law would give two different answers for B depending on whether the Amperian loop's surface crossed the wire or the gap. With the correction, both give the same answer. The displacement current "completes the circuit" through the changing electric field.

Punchline: A changing electric field acts magnetically just like a real current. This term seemed like bookkeeping at first. It turned out to be the key to everything that followed: it lets electromagnetic waves exist.

🌊 The Payoff — Light Itself

Combine Faraday and Ampère-Maxwell in empty space (no charges, no currents). A changing E field makes a B field. That changing B field makes an E field. That changing E field makes a B field. The two fields feed each other and propagate outward as a self-sustaining wave.

Working out the wave equation from the four laws, Maxwell found the speed of the wave was:

c = 1 / √(μ₀ · ε₀) = 1 / √(4π×10⁻⁷ × 8.854×10⁻¹²) ≈ 2.998 × 10⁸ m/s
Plug in the experimentally measured values of μ₀ and ε₀ — both measured in laboratory experiments on static electricity and magnetism, with no light involved — and out pops the speed of light.

This was the moment electromagnetism and optics merged. Maxwell wrote: "We can scarcely avoid the inference that light consists in the transverse undulations of the same medium which is the cause of electric and magnetic phenomena." He was right. Light is an electromagnetic wave. Radio is too. So are microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays. The only difference between a radio wave and gamma ray is wavelength.

The Equations, Together

Maxwell's full set in integral form:

∮ E · dA = Q / ε₀ (Gauss — electricity)
∮ B · dA = 0 (Gauss — no monopoles)
∮ E · dℓ = − dΦ_B / dt (Faraday)
∮ B · dℓ = μ₀ I + μ₀ε₀ dΦ_E / dt (Ampère-Maxwell)
Four lines. Every classical electromagnetic phenomenon in the universe is contained in them. Special relativity falls out almost for free, because these equations are already relativistically correct as written — Einstein noticed.

Richard Feynman once wrote that ten thousand years from now, when civilizations have come and gone, the discovery that will still matter from the nineteenth century is Maxwell's discovery of the laws of electromagnetism. That is the scale of the achievement you have been playing with.
